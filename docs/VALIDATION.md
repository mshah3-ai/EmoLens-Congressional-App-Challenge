# Validation study (simulator results)

Every number here comes from a real run of the simulator in `packages/simulator`. The run used the exact engine
code the app will use. The source of each number is the `summary.json` files copied to `docs/validation-data/`
(the full CSVs are regenerated by the command below). **These are simulated families, not real children.** The
study shows what the math can and cannot do when we know the right answer. It does not prove anything about real
families (see Section 5).

## 1. Setup
- **Scenarios:** `packages/simulator/scenarios/default.json`, exactly as in SPEC 26.1:
  - base chance 5% per block, afternoon +0.3
  - planted triggers: `sleep_deficit` 0.35, `planned_transitions` 0.6, `missed_dose` 0.7, `illness` 0.8 (logit per
    feature unit; each family's effects are these × U(0.7, 1.3))
  - every other trigger is truly 0
  - the parent suspects `sleep_deficit` (right) and `high_sugar_recent` (wrong)
  - caregiver behavior: logs 90% of hard moments, end-of-day check 80%, answers 70% of alerts, steps in after 50% of
    alerts, stepping in halves the chance

  E6 uses `scenarios/physiology.json` (the same, plus heart-rate data and a true `hr_elevated_pre` effect of 0.5).
- **Families × days:** 200 families × 120 days in every arm (16 distinct settings, so 3,200 simulated families of
  120 days each). Start 2026-09-07 in America/New_York (the run crosses the 2026-11-01 DST change).
- **Seed:** 20260928. The same seed is used in every arm, so arms compare the *same* simulated children (common
  random numbers, see ARCHITECTURE_EXPLAINED Section 4).
- **Commands:**
  ```
  pnpm --filter @emolens/simulator sim --scenario default --families 200 --days 120 --out out
  python analysis/plots.py --in packages/simulator/out --figures docs/figures
  python analysis/fig1_publication.py --csv packages/simulator/out/E1/default/daily_metrics.csv --out docs/figures/fig1_publication
  ```
  The full study took 621 s on 14 CPU cores (569 s the first time). The engine averaged 17 ms per family per
  simulated day.
- **Engine code:** the commit that updates this file on 2026-10-24 (`git log -- docs/VALIDATION.md`). It includes both
  tuning changes in Section 4: the τ grid (2026-09-28) and "setting A" for the `likely` status (2026-10-24). All
  results in Sections 2 and 3 are from the 2026-10-24 re-run with setting A. The earlier results are kept in Section 4.
- **What setting A changes:** only the rule that decides when a pattern is shown as "likely". Forecasts, alerts,
  calibration and coefficient estimates don't depend on that rule, and came out identical (all 3,890 non-status
  numbers in the summaries matched the earlier run).
- **How the app is simulated:** every evening at 18:00 the real `runEngine` refits using only what the app could
  know by then. It forecasts tomorrow's blocks, and the real `planAlerts` schedules tomorrow's alerts.
- **How forecasts are scored:** the metrics for day *d* use the day-ahead forecasts for days *d*+1 … *d*+7. They are
  scored against what *really* happened (logged or not), leaving out blocks where the parent stepped in (SPEC 26.3,
  DECISIONS #17).

## 2. Acceptance targets (SPEC 26.6) and actual results

| Target | Actual (default scenario, 200 families) | Met? |
|---|---|---|
| Mean detection rate of planted triggers by day 90 ≥ 0.70 | **0.841** (95% CI 0.815–0.867); day 120: 0.894 (0.874–0.914) | **Yes** |
| `likely`/`strong` rate among null features ≤ 0.05 at every day | **Highest daily mean 0.042** (day 65) | **Yes** (0.055 with the spec's settings, 0.035 after the τ change, 0.042 with setting A; Section 4) |
| Skill score > 0 in ≥ 80% of families by day 45 | **64.5%** of families (next-7-days window at day 45) | **No** |
| E2: naive labeling lowers mean β̂ of planted triggers, 95% CI excludes 0 at intervene prob 0.5 | EmoLens − naive = **+0.0056** logit (95% CI +0.0043 to +0.0071) at day 90; +0.0079 (+0.0062 to +0.0097) at day 120 | **Yes** (but the effect is small, see E2) |

**Confirmation on a fresh seed.** Setting A was chosen on separate tuning families (seeds 101–104) and then checked
once on 200 *new* families (seed 20261022): detection **0.853** (95% CI 0.830–0.875) at day 90 and **0.880**
(0.859–0.901) at day 120, worst daily null likely/strong rate **0.043** (day 73). The shipped code reproduces that run
exactly (`validation-data/tuning-2026-10-22/confirm_seed20261022_settingA.summary.json`). On the same new families the
earlier settings found 0.774 at day 90 (Section 4).

**Why the skill target is missed, honestly.**
- A 7-day window holds about 56 blocks and only ~4 hard moments, so one family's skill score is mostly coin-flip
  noise.
- An *oracle* forecaster that knows the true chance of every block beats the time-of-day guess on those 7 days in
  only **72%** of families at day 45. That oracle is impossible to build, because it even knows tomorrow's missed
  dose and last night's sleep before they happen.
- So with this scenario and this metric, 80% cannot be reached by *any* forecaster. We did not change the metric to
  pass.
- Using the *expected* Brier score (scored against the true chance, which removes the coin-flip noise), EmoLens's
  skill is positive in **78%** of families at day 45 and **81.5%** at day 90.
- Its average expected skill is small (+0.013 at day 45) compared with the oracle's ceiling (+0.043).
- Most of the planted signal cannot be known a day ahead. Tomorrow's sleep, tomorrow morning's dose and a cold that
  starts tomorrow are all unknown at 18:00 the evening before. Only planned transitions and an illness that has
  already started are.

## 3. Experiments

### E1: How fast does EmoLens learn?
Default scenario. The simulated children averaged **5.08** hard moments a week (95% CI 4.99–5.17), and the
simulated parents logged 89.8% of them.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 | 0.03 | 0.005 | 0.002 | 1.00 (fixed) |
| 30 | 0.475 | 0.08 | 0.018 | 0.99 |
| 45 | 0.665 | 0.11 | 0.032 | 0.88 |
| 60 | 0.745 | 0.11 | 0.040 | 0.85 |
| 90 | 0.841 | 0.13 | 0.036 | 0.81 |
| 120 | 0.894 | 0.13 | 0.036 | 0.79 |

- **Which planted triggers are found (share of families showing likely/strong; seed 20260928):**

| Planted trigger | Day 90 | Day 120 | What the others showed at day 90 |
|---|---|---|---|
| sleep_deficit | 0.96 | 0.975 | "possible" 4, "still checking" 4 (of 200) |
| planned_transitions | 0.865 | 0.93 | "possible" 16, "no clear link" 11 |
| missed_dose | **0.715** | **0.76** | "possible" 41, "still checking" 12, "not enough data" 3, "no clear link" 1 |
| illness | 0.825 | 0.91 | "possible" 22, "not enough data" 9, "still checking" 4 |

  Missed doses are still missed most: a dose is missed about 1 day in 14, so by day 90 many families have only a few
  hard moments right after one, and the cautious prior keeps P(raises risk) under 0.90 (Section 4). Setting A raised
  missed doses from 0.65 to 0.715, illness from 0.75 to 0.825 and planned transitions from 0.78 to 0.865 at day 90.
- **Forecast quality.** AUROC (ranking risky vs calm blocks) was 0.56 at day 30 and 0.60 at day 90, with all
  families pooled; 0.5 is a coin flip. Calibration is good. Forecasts made after day 30 average 8.4%, against a
  true average of 9.2%. The observed rate per forecast bin is shown in fig3: when EmoLens said about 7%, hard
  moments happened 8.1% of the time; when it said about 13%, 12.1%.
- **Coefficient recovery (day 90, mean over 200 families; true values are family-specific):**

| Planted trigger | Mean true β | Mean estimated β | Bias | RMSE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sleep_deficit (per hour) | 0.355 | 0.360 | +0.005 | 0.099 |
| planned_transitions | 0.599 | 0.335 | −0.264 | 0.290 |
| missed_dose | 0.680 | 0.383 | −0.297 | 0.339 |
| illness | 0.784 | 0.538 | −0.247 | 0.289 |

  - The larger effects are under-estimated by about 0.25–0.3. The Bayesian prior pulls them toward the registry's
    starting guesses (0.20–0.50), and only weeks of rare exposures (7% missed doses, a cold about once a month) can
    pull them away.
  - Truly-null triggers are pulled toward their (positive) starting guesses too. At day 120, `unusual_day` averages
    +0.22, `new_place` +0.23 and `appointment_near` +0.21, against starting guesses of 0.25–0.30.
  - This is the price of cautious priors: patterns appear slower, and are shown as "possible" rather than "likely"
    for longer.
- **Alerts.** 0.79 alerts per family per week. 276 were suppressed by quiet hours, 384 by the 3-hour cooldown and
  44 by the daily maximum. Parents answered 71.5% of alerts; 44% of the answers were "we stepped in". The hit rate
  parents would see (happened ÷ (happened + calm)) was 0.27. A hard moment actually followed 15% of alerts. That is
  about 1.7× the 9% average block, and lower than 0.27 partly because stepping in halved the chance.

### E2: Does the three-answer confirmation avoid the intervention trap?
Same children, two labeling rules. EmoLens leaves "we stepped in" blocks out of the risk model. The naive rule
counts them as calm.

| Parents step in after | Mean β̂ of planted triggers, EmoLens − naive (day 120, 95% bootstrap CI) | Alerts per week, EmoLens vs naive |
|---|---|---|
| 30% of alerts | +0.0049 (+0.0037 to +0.0062) | 0.78 vs 0.72 |
| 50% of alerts | +0.0079 (+0.0062 to +0.0097) | 0.79 vs 0.70 |
| 80% of alerts | +0.0124 (+0.0101 to +0.0148) | 0.81 vs 0.68 |

- The trap is real and grows with how often parents act on alerts. Naive labeling makes the model under-estimate
  the planted triggers, and its forecasts become less alarmed: 16% fewer alerts at 80% stepping in.
- In this scenario the effect is **small**, about 1–3% of the planted effects. With about 0.8 alerts a week,
  70% answered and 44–71% of answers being "stepped in", a family collects only about 3–7 mislabeled blocks in 120
  days (about 13 alerts × 71.5% answered × the stepping-in share).
- The AUROC difference was not distinguishable from 0 (for example +0.0005, 95% CI −0.0012 to +0.0021, at day 90 for 50%). The
  detection difference was not significant either.
- The trap would matter more for a family that gets more alerts (for example the "more alerts" sensitivity setting
  or early alerts), and over a longer time.

> **fig4:** Top: estimated ÷ true effect, averaged over the planted triggers, for EmoLens labels (blue) and naive
> labels (orange), at three stepping-in rates. Bottom: the paired difference for the same children (EmoLens minus
> naive) with its 95% interval. It stays above 0 and grows over time.

### E3: Does shrinkage prevent fake patterns?

| Setting (day 90) | Planted found | Null "possible" | Null likely/strong | Worst day, null likely/strong | Expected skill | Forecast ÷ true chance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Empirical-Bayes τ (EmoLens) | 0.841 | 0.127 | 0.036 | 0.042 | +0.012 | 0.92 |
| Fixed τ = 1 | 0.763 | 0.089 | 0.026 | 0.031 | +0.012 | 0.94 |
| Weak prior (sd 5, τ = 1) | 0.578 | 0.067 | 0.051 | **0.055** | −0.012 (−0.10 at day 30) | 1.15 |

- **The prior is what prevents fake patterns and bad forecasts.** With a weak prior, fake likely/strong patterns
  appear earlier (0.037 at day 30 vs 0.018) and break the 0.05 limit (0.055), and far fewer real patterns are found
  (0.58 vs 0.84 at day 90).
- With a weak prior, forecasts are *worse than the simple time-of-day guess* for the first two months: expected
  skill is −0.10 at day 30, and only 26.5% of families have positive skill at day 45.
- **Choosing τ from the data did not reduce fake patterns compared with τ = 1.** It raised both real detection
  (0.84 vs 0.76) and fake "possible" patterns (0.13 vs 0.09).
  - The reason: τ below 1 narrows each starting guess but keeps its center. Since most starting guesses are
    slightly positive, the evidence tips toward "raises risk" for both real and null triggers.
  - Both settings stay under the 0.05 limit. This is also why τ = 0.5 was removed (Section 4).

> **fig5:** Share of null triggers shown as likely/strong (left) or possible (middle), and share of planted
> triggers found (right), for the three prior settings.

### E4: How much does logging diligence matter?

| Logs hard moments | End-of-day check | Planted found (day 90) | Forecast ÷ true chance | Expected skill (day 90) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100% | 90% | 0.871 | 0.91 | +0.011 |
| 100% | 60% | 0.841 | 1.32 | +0.044 |
| 80% | 90% | 0.831 | 0.74 | +0.002 |
| 80% | 60% | 0.814 | 1.09 | +0.023 |
| 60% | 90% | 0.780 | 0.58 | −0.003 |
| 60% | 60% | 0.751 | 0.86 | +0.007 |

- **Logging every hard moment matters most.** Pattern detection at day 90 falls from 0.87 to 0.78 when only 60%
  are logged. Skipping end-of-day checks costs less (0.87 → 0.84). Fake likely/strong patterns stayed at or under
  0.041 on every day in all six settings.
- The two kinds of missing data push forecasts in opposite directions:
  - An **unlogged** hard moment on a checked day becomes a false "calm" label, so forecasts run low (58% of the true
    chance at 60% logging).
  - A **skipped end-of-day check** leaves only the day's logged hard moments as labels, with no calm blocks, so
    forecasts run high (132% at 60% checks).
- The skill score is relative to a time-of-day model trained on the same biased labels. It moves in ways that are
  hard to interpret (skill is *higher* with fewer checks), so we treat detection and calibration as the real
  diligence measures.
- A useful finding for the app: remind parents to log *every* hard moment, even briefly. The one-tap log exists for
  exactly this.

> **fig6:** Planted triggers found (top) and expected skill (bottom), by logging rate, with the end-of-day check
> done 90% (left) or 60% (right) of days.

### E5: Can the data overrule a wrong parent belief?
In the default scenario the parent suspects sugary snacks (`high_sugar_recent`, truly no effect) and short sleep
(truly raises risk). Both get a +0.15 head start in the prior.

| Day | Sugary snack: shown as likely/strong | possible | "no clear link" | still checking / not enough data | Short sleep: likely/strong |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 0% | 2% | 0% | 98% | 86% |
| 60 | 0% | 10.5% | 0.5% | 89% | 94.5% |
| 90 | 1% | 14% | 4% | 81% | 96% |
| 120 | 0.5% | 12.5% | 19% | 68% | 97.5% |

- The data confirms the right belief quickly: short sleep reaches likely/strong in 86% of families by day 30.
- The wrong belief is almost never promoted (at most 1% of families at any milestone, the same as before setting A).
- The data is slow to actively *clear* the wrong belief. Sugary snacks are rare (a sugary snack about one day in
  seven), so by day 120 only 19% of families see "No clear link"; most still see "Still checking".
- 12.5% of families still see "Possible link" at day 120, partly because of the head start.

### E6: Does physiology help?
The same children (with a true "heart rate in the hour before" effect of 0.5 per unit) were simulated twice. In one
run the app uses heart-rate data; in the other it does not.

- **It finds the pattern.** "Heart rate before" is shown as likely/strong in 80.5% of families at day 90 and 88% at
  day 120. Overall planted-trigger detection is 0.832 with heart-rate data vs 0.690 without; without the data, that
  trigger can never be found.
- **It does not improve day-ahead forecasts.** By design (SPEC 12.7), a day-ahead forecast cannot know tomorrow's
  pre-block heart rate, so it sets that feature to 0.
  - Pooled AUROC at day 90: 0.597 with vs 0.598 without. Paired AUROC difference +0.001 (−0.005 to +0.007).
  - Expected skill was very slightly *lower* with the extra feature (−0.0009, 95% CI −0.0014 to −0.0004 at day 90).
  - Physiology could only help the "next block" forecast made during the day, which this study does not simulate.

> **fig8:** AUROC (left) and expected skill (middle) of day-ahead forecasts with and without heart-rate data (the
> oracle ceiling is in green). Right: share of families where "heart rate before" is shown as likely/strong.

## 4. Tuning log
Only the parameters allowed by SPEC 26.6 may be tuned (status thresholds, `REASON_MIN_CONTRIBUTION`, `TAU_GRID`,
registry prior sds). Each change and its effect is recorded here. Each tuning run below used the default scenario,
200 families × 120 days, seed 20260928.

| Date | Parameter | Old | New | Effect | Kept? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-28 | (baseline, spec values) | | | Detection @90 0.755; worst-day null likely/strong **0.055** (day 110, misses ≤ 0.05); null possible 0.16; skill > 0 @45 in 65.5% of families | |
| 2026-09-28 | `TAU_GRID` | [0.5, 0.75, 1.0, 1.5] | [0.75, 1.0, 1.5] | Detection @90 0.755 → **0.785**; worst-day null likely/strong 0.055 → **0.035**; null possible 0.16 → 0.13; skill share @45 65.5% → 64.5% (noise level) | **Yes** |
| 2026-09-28 | `STATUS_THRESHOLDS.likely.pHarm` (tried alone, with the original τ grid) | 0.90 | 0.95 | Worst-day null likely/strong 0.035, but detection @90 fell to 0.725 and null "possible" rose to 0.19 | No (reverted) |
| 2026-10-24 | `STATUS_THRESHOLDS.likely` exposed / exposedPos / or (setting A, searched 2026-10-22) | 20 / 5 / 1.3 | 15 / 3 / 1.25 | Detection @90 0.785 → **0.841**, @120 0.846 → **0.894**; worst-day null likely/strong 0.035 → **0.042**; null possible @90 0.13 → 0.13; forecasts unchanged. Confirmation seed: 0.853 @90, worst 0.043 | **Yes** |

**Why `TAU_GRID` was the right lever:**
- Before tuning, the false "likely" patterns were almost all on triggers with the largest *starting guesses*:
  `unusual_day` (prior mean 0.30), `appointment_near` and `new_place` (0.25), and `hunger_gap`.
- With τ = 0.5 the prior sd is halved but the prior mean is not. For `unusual_day` the prior *alone* then gives
  P(raises risk) = Φ(0.30 / 0.175) ≈ 0.96, above the 0.90 needed for "likely".
- Removing τ = 0.5 fixes the cause instead of hiding it with a stricter threshold. It also found more real patterns.
- A deeper fix would shrink toward 0 instead of toward the positive starting guesses. That changes the spec's model
  (13.3), not a tunable, so it is noted here for the student to consider.

### 2026-10-22: trying to raise day-90 detection toward 0.86–0.87 (setting A; adopted on 2026-10-24)
**Goal:** raise detection of planted triggers at day 90 from 0.785 toward 0.86–0.87, using only what SPEC 26.6 allows,
with the false likely/strong rate at or below 0.05 on **every** day. The generator, the scoring and every reported
number were left alone. If no setting reached 0.86–0.87 within the limit, the original settings would be kept.

**Method.**
- The search tool `packages/simulator/src/tune.ts` runs the default scenario once per candidate setting on the same
  families, and reports detection, the worst day's false rate and *why* each planted trigger was missed.
- The search used small runs on **tuning seeds 101–104** (50 families each), never the confirmation seed.
- The one chosen setting was then confirmed with a full 200-family run (the normal `sim` command, `--seed 20261022`).
- Only settings that apply to every trigger the same way were tried. A trigger's own prior sd was not changed because
  we know it is planted; that would fit the answer instead of the method.
- `REASON_MIN_CONTRIBUTION` was not tried: it only decides which reasons are shown next to a forecast, and the status
  rules never read it, so it can't change detection.
- The settings and results are in `validation-data/tuning-2026-10-22/`.

**Why triggers were missed (day 90, original settings, 200 tuning families):**
- Missed dose: in 42 families the "at least 5 exposed hard moments" rule blocked "likely", in 14 "at least 20 exposed
  blocks", and in 23 P(raises risk) < 0.90.
- Illness: 21 families were blocked by the 5-hard-moment rule, 4 by the 20-block rule, 9 had too few illness blocks
  yet ("not enough data") and 6 had P(raises risk) < 0.90.
- Planned transitions: 24 families were blocked by odds ratio < 1.3 and 13 by P(raises risk) < 0.90.

- **Lowering P(raises risk) or adding a smaller τ** finds more triggers, but also turns null triggers with a positive
  starting guess (`unusual_day`, `appointment_near`, `new_place`) into fake "likely" patterns, well past 0.05.
- **Wider priors** (bigger τ or sds) find *fewer* triggers: the cautious positive starting guesses were helping.
- **The exposure minimums were the main lever.** Rare triggers simply don't have 5 hard moments right after them by
  day 90. Lowering those minimums found more without many more fake patterns, because "likely" still needs
  P(raises risk) ≥ 0.90.

**Chosen before confirming:** setting A (`likely`: exposed ≥ 15, exposedPos ≥ 3, OR ≥ 1.25; `pHarm` 0.90 and every
other rule unchanged). It had the highest detection that still left a margin under 0.05. The 1.2 version was only
0.003 below the limit on the tuning families.

## 5. Limitations
- **Simulated, not real.** The children, their triggers and the parents' behavior are made up. Real children have
  triggers the model does not measure, and effects that change over time and interact. Real parents' logging
  mistakes are not random.
- **The simulator uses the engine's own feature definitions for the truth.** This tests whether the model can
  recover effects that are expressed in its own features. It cannot test whether those features are the right way
  to measure a real trigger.
- **Only one daily run.** The simulated app refits once a day at 18:00 and only day-ahead forecasts are scored.
  In-day "next block" forecasts, morning outlooks and the recompute-on-every-log behavior are not simulated.
- **Perfect weather forecast, no pollen, no steps data, no food-item analysis** ("worth watching" is skipped in the
  simulator for speed).
- **Effect sizes and rates are the spec's choices.** Weaker or rarer triggers would take longer to detect; more
  alerts would make the intervention trap matter more.
- **Skill scores over 7 days are very noisy** for a single family with ~5 hard moments a week (Section 2). Model
  Health in the app therefore waits for 20 evaluable predictions and uses 28-day windows.
